Human 1.0: Water:Fat in the breast

Summary

Human 1.0 shows that the difference between NORMAL and CANCER breasts is in the water-rich scans. Measured there, a basic analysis points to the CANCER breast rather than the NORMAL one, matching synchrotron and ex-vivo data on the water-rich side.

What we expect: a normal breast is mostly fat with some glandular/fibrous tissue. Cancer replaces fat with cells and remodelled fibrous tissue, which hold more water. Each breast gets one number, its Water:Fat ratio: the area of its water peak divided by the area of its fat peak, from two fitted Gaussians. Comparing a patient’s two breasts, the CANCER breast should have the higher Water:Fat.

Data: 258 patients analysed with Human 1.0 (NORMAL, BENIGN or CANCER); 251 used, after leaving out 7 with a large left/right intensity imbalance (Appendix I).

1. The difference is in the water-rich scans

In the fattiest 15% of samples the three cohorts look the same; in the wettest 15% they separate NORMAL < BENIGN < CANCER. The information is in the water-rich tissue — denser breasts are better for us; mammography’s weakness is our strength (Chapter 1).

2. It matches other data

That wettest-15% ordering matches synchrotron and Keele ex-vivo data. A fixed water-peak threshold for single-point scans (Appendix C) also keeps the wet tissue; the paired test there supports CANCER vs NORMAL (Chapter 2).

3. Rigorous tumour targeting is not needed

The tumours are small and only a few beams are placed approximately, yet the signal holds where the beam goes through glandular tissue: changes appear wide-reaching in the glandular breast, not confined to the tumour (Chapter 3).

4. Mapping for Human 2.0 Proposal

Scan a grid over the whole breast and select the wettest tissue after the scan. The water-peak rule (Appendix C) could be a stopgap on Human 1.0; mapping is the recommendation (Chapter 4). A simulated half breast at 5 mm steps, one second per point, finishes in 2 min 46 s (Chapter 5).

Code 
# top row: both breasts usable; bottom row: both breasts have rejected (fatty) scans only
EXAMPLES = ((266, False), (439, False), (506, True), (250, True))  # (nova, use rejected scans)
found_use = rule_breasts(WATER_MIN, CENTRE_MIN)
found_rej = rule_breasts(WATER_MIN, CENTRE_MIN, rejected=True)
plt.rcParams["font.size"] = 7
fig, axes = plt.subplots(2, 2, figsize=(8, 5.2), sharex=True, sharey=True)
for ax, (nova, rejected) in zip(axes.flat, EXAMPLES):
    found = found_rej if rejected else found_use
    labels = []
    for side, style in (("LEFT", "-"), ("RIGHT", "--")):
        label, ratio, n_kept, _ = found[nova, side]
        curves = [curve for n, s, _, curve, height, centre in scan_records
                  if n == nova and s == side and usable(height, centre, WATER_MIN, CENTRE_MIN) != rejected]
        with warnings.catch_warnings():
            warnings.simplefilter("ignore", RuntimeWarning)
            ax.plot(q, np.nanmean(curves, axis=0), color=ne.STATUS_COLOURS[label], ls=style, lw=1.6,
                    label=f"{side} {label}, Water:Fat {ratio:.2f}")
        labels.append(label)
    ax.axvspan(*WATER, color="grey", alpha=0.12, lw=0)
    ax.hlines(WATER_MIN, *WATER, color="black", lw=1.5, zorder=5)
    kind = "fatty scans" if rejected else "water-rich scans"
    ax.set(title=f"{' / '.join(labels)}: {kind}", xlim=(2, 25))
    ax.grid(alpha=0.3)
    ax.legend(frameon=False, fontsize=6, loc="upper left")
for ax in axes[-1]:
    ax.set_xlabel("q (nm⁻¹)")
for ax in axes[:, 0]:
    ax.set_ylabel("I(q), prepared")
fig.tight_layout()
plt.show()
plt.rcParams["font.size"] = 10

A pilot result: 18 CANCER/NORMAL patients, 95% CI 59%–96%, and mostly dense breasts. All limitations are in Chapter 6.
